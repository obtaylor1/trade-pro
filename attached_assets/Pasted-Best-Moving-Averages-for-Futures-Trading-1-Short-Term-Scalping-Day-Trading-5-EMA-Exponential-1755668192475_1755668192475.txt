Best Moving Averages for Futures Trading
1. Short-Term (Scalping & Day Trading)
* 5 EMA (Exponential Moving Average):
    * Tracks price very closely, reacts fast.
    * Good for spotting very short-term momentum.
* 9 EMA / 10 EMA:
    * Slightly smoother than 5 EMA.
    * Common on 1-min, 5-min, or 15-min charts to confirm trend direction.
* 20 EMA:
    * Strong for intraday trend confirmation.
    * Often used as a dynamic support/resistance line in day trading futures like ES (S&P 500) or CL (crude oil).
👉 Use Case: If price is above the 9/20 EMA combo, trend is bullish; below, trend is bearish.

2. Medium-Term (Swing Trading Futures)
* 50 SMA (Simple Moving Average):
    * Classic “swing trend” average.
    * Many traders buy pullbacks near the 50 SMA in trending markets.
* 21 EMA:
    * Faster than the 50 SMA; popular with swing traders who want quicker entries.
* 34 EMA:
    * Commonly used in forex & futures swing systems for smoother trend following.
👉 Use Case: In swing trading, look for price bouncing off the 21/34/50 MA to ride a multi-day move.

3. Long-Term (Position Trading & Big Trends)
* 100 SMA:
    * Tracks intermediate trends; often acts as a “major line in the sand.”
* 200 SMA (or EMA):
    * The most-watched average by institutions.
    * Futures like ES (S&P), NQ (Nasdaq), GC (Gold), and CL (Crude Oil) often respect the 200-day average.
    * Above 200 = long-term uptrend; below 200 = long-term downtrend.
👉 Use Case: Position traders hold futures long above 200 SMA and look for shorts below it.

4. Specialty Techniques
* 9/21 EMA Cross: Popular for short-term momentum trades.
* Golden Cross / Death Cross (50 SMA vs 200 SMA): Used for spotting long-term trend reversals.
* Hull Moving Average (HMA): Smooth but very responsive — used by some algo futures traders.
* VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price): Not a traditional moving average, but widely used intraday in futures to gauge fair value.

✅ Summary of Best Moving Averages in Futures
* Day trading: 5 EMA, 9 EMA, 20 EMA (fast reaction).
* Swing trading: 21 EMA, 34 EMA, 50 SMA (trend pullbacks).
* Position trading: 100 SMA, 200 SMA (big-picture trend).
* Advanced: VWAP, Hull MA for intraday strategies.


📊 Futures Trading Strategy: EMA Cross + SMA Filter
Indicators Used
* 9 EMA (Exponential Moving Average) → fast, short-term momentum
* 21 EMA → medium-term trend stabilizer
* 200 SMA (Simple Moving Average) → long-term trend filter

Step 1: Define Market Bias with 200 SMA
* If price is above the 200 SMA → Only take long trades.
* If price is below the 200 SMA → Only take short trades. 👉 This prevents fighting the overall trend.

Step 2: Entry Signal (9/21 EMA Cross)
* Bullish Entry (Long):
    * Price above 200 SMA
    * 9 EMA crosses above 21 EMA
    * Enter on next candle close or slight pullback
* Bearish Entry (Short):
    * Price below 200 SMA
    * 9 EMA crosses below 21 EMA

Step 3: Risk Management
* Place stop loss 1–2 points below recent swing low for longs (above swing high for shorts).
* Risk no more than 1–2% of your account on a single trade.

Step 4: Take Profits
* Partial exit at +1R (profit equal to your risk).
* Let remaining contracts ride until:
    * 9 EMA crosses back under 21 EMA (for longs), or
    * Price touches a key level (previous high/low, VWAP).

Step 5: Example Trade on ES (Intraday)
* Price at 4500, trading above 200 SMA (bias = bullish).
* 9 EMA crosses above 21 EMA at 4502.
* Enter long at 4503.
* Stop loss at 4498 (5-point risk = $250 per contract).
* Target 1: 4508 (+5 points = $250 profit).
* Target 2: 4515 (+12 points = $600 profit).
* If 9 EMA later crosses back below 21 EMA, exit remainder.

✅ Why This Works
* 200 SMA keeps you aligned with the big trend.
* 9/21 EMA cross gives timely entries with momentum.
* Scaling out profits ensures you lock in gains while letting winners run.
